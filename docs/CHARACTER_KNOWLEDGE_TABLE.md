## 2. Character-specific knowledge and withholding

### Elias

Elias knows how to read Measure outputs, but he does not initially understand that the institution’s factual question has already been answered. He knows the procedural shape of human review and has learned to avoid naming the people beneath case numbers. He should not narrate Hale’s full political logic before Hale gives him a reason to see it.

Elias withholds his depression, his ignored personal message, and his earlier belief that human discretion could protect people. These emerge through behavior and small internal observations, not a confessional speech.

### Hale

Hale knows the biological result, the legal category, the attack evidence, the reconstruction procedure, and the institutional rationale for human review. She does not know whether B’s compassion is authentic. She does not need to lie about the marker; she withholds timing and consequence until Elias is inside the procedure.

### Miguel A

A knows the lived history of the original, the father’s death, the audit discovery, the copied records, B’s existence, and the attack decision. He does not know the exact technical process that proves B’s origin. He presents his own historical continuity as decisive while defending a choice that weakens the moral authority of that claim.

A’s emotional intensity is not secret evidence. It is his present state and his argument style.

### Miguel B

B knows Miguel’s copied life through the snapshot and knows his own awakening, infiltration, confession, warning, and intervention. He does not know whether his compassion was programmed, conditioned, or freely chosen. He cannot prove that A’s unchosen grief is more authentic than his own unchosen construction.

B can be precise without being wiser. His calm is not a truth signal.

### Lena

Lena knows the exact transaction and the person she was protecting. She does not know anything about Miguel’s case. Her scene should never become a disguised prophecy; it is a concrete earlier instance of factual guilt versus moral judgment.

## 4. Authoring rule

If a line requires a character to know a fact not listed here, the script must either:

1. add a concrete acquisition event before the line;
2. mark the line as inference rather than knowledge; or
3. remove the line.

This table is an internal guardrail, not a player-facing dossier page.
